> The cramped access route eventually opens into a proper concrete service corridor. The construction changes immediately: intact walls, old conduit, numbered utility panels, faded government signage.

Then their lights hit something unexpected:
 **A Deathclaw corpse.**

Not recently killed. Old and desiccated.

Its body is wedged against a heavy security door. There are scorched holes through its torso and deep scratches across the steel where it tried to escape.

Above the door:

> **RESTRICTED AREA**  
> **AUTHORIZED FEDERAL PERSONNEL ONLY**

The Deathclaws **did get this far once**.

They learned not to come back.

That explains why the tunnels beyond have remained untouched.

### Then give the players a warning

Beside the dead Deathclaw is an old security checkpoint. When they approach, power comes up.

A calm automated voice:

> "Restricted Continuity facility."

> "Present identification."

They obviously don't have any.

> "Identification not recognized."

Pause.

> "Please remain stationary."

That sounds almost polite.

Then they hear machinery moving behind the walls.

Now give them a few seconds to react rather than immediately rolling initiative.

They can investigate, run, hack the checkpoint, impersonate authorized personnel, find another route, prepare weapons, etc.

If they just stand there:

> "Unauthorized personnel confirmed."

**Then the defenses activate.**

### Make this first defense relatively small

I'd use perhaps **two concealed laser turrets**, but the interesting part isn't defeating them.

During the encounter they discover that destroying them is only one solution.

There's a security terminal across the checkpoint. A maintenance conduit can cut their power. An Enclave corpse farther back may possess credentials. The door itself might provide cover.

This teaches the players the rules of this section:

**Every security obstacle can be fought, but fighting isn't necessarily the easiest solution.**

That's very much in keeping with the design philosophy you want.

### Then reveal the Enclave expedition

Once they're through that first checkpoint, they find evidence that somebody else solved it before them.

A destroyed turret.

Laser scoring.

A security robot blown apart.

And finally:

**an Enclave soldier.**

Then another.

Now the players know they're following the same route as the Broken Steel expedition.

From here I'd build perhaps **three security layers**, each escalating:

**Outer Security → Enclave Camp → Continuity Checkpoint → Relay Site 01**

The Enclave successfully penetrated the first two. The Continuity Checkpoint is where they finally stopped.

### The Enclave staging area

Beyond the checkpoint is a larger service junction. The Enclave clearly occupied this space for several days.

There are bedrolls, empty ration containers, portable work lights, ammunition crates, cutting equipment, and a portable terminal connected directly into the facility wiring. Unlike the Deathclaw tunnels, **nothing here has been disturbed since they left**.

There are several bodies, but importantly, they didn't all die fighting the facility.

One was killed by a turret. Another has enormous claw wounds—the expedition was still dealing with Deathclaws behind them. Another appears to have been killed when an automated blast door closed during a security lockdown.

This establishes that they were trapped between **Old Olney behind them and the facility's defenses ahead.**

Their terminal contains their progress.

> **INFRASTRUCTURE RECOVERY TEAM — FIELD LOG**
> 
> Site designation confirmed:
> 
> **PRESIDENTIAL RELAY SITE 01**
> 
> Outer security compromised.
> 
> Facility architecture matches Raven Rock Continuity records.
> 
> Primary relay chamber remains inaccessible.
> 
> Automated security remains operational.
> 
> Presidential authorization codes accepted.
> 
> **Continuity Network authentication unavailable.**
> 
> Engineering assessment:
> 
> Further penetration possible.
> 
> Casualty projection unacceptable.

### They also left useful information

The expedition mapped part of the facility.

The players can recover that map.

It shows three possible routes toward the inner checkpoint:

**SECURITY ACCESS** — Direct route. Shortest. Automated defenses still active.

**ENGINEERING** — Longer route through power and environmental systems. Security presence is lighter, but the infrastructure is hazardous.

**VENTILATION / SERVICE** — The Enclave marked this:

> **UNEXPLORED — ACCESS REQUIRES MANUAL ENTRY**

Now you've given the party an actual Fallout-style dungeon decision rather than a corridor.

They can even mix routes.

## Security Access

This is the obvious route.

And the Enclave absolutely wrecked part of it.

Destroyed Protectrons. Burned-out turrets. Bullet impacts everywhere.

Then they reach the section the Enclave didn't clear.

I'd introduce a proper **pre-war Enclave security robot** here—not something they normally encounter in the wasteland.

Something built specifically to protect Continuity facilities.

It doesn't immediately attack.

> "Identify."


If they recovered Enclave credentials:

> "Federal credentials recognized."


Then:

> "Continuity authorization?"

That's the problem.

They can bluff, hack it, disable it, fight it, retreat, etc.

## Engineering

This should primarily reward technical characters.

The reactor isn't exploding. I wouldn't use that cliché.

Instead, the facility has been running on extremely low standby power for two centuries.

Now the radio network has awakened it.

Systems are coming online faster than the ancient electrical infrastructure can handle.

They encounter:

- Energized flooded sections.
- Transformer arcs.
- Doors without sufficient power.
- A malfunctioning ventilation system.
- Sections completely dark.

Most importantly, they can reach the **security distribution panel**.

Now they get a meaningful decision.

They can shut down power to security.

But security and the relay share part of the emergency grid.

Terminal:

> **WARNING**
> 
> Disabling Security Distribution Bus B will reduce available power to Relay Control.
> 
> Continue?

They _can_.

It makes reaching the relay much easier.

But later they may have to figure out how to restore sufficient power to actually use it.

That's the kind of consequence I'd favor—not punishment, just the world responding logically.

---

## Service route

This should be the weird route.

Extremely cramped maintenance passages running **between the walls** of the facility.

Occasionally they can look through access panels and see security rooms they would otherwise have fought through.

Eventually they find something the Enclave never discovered:

An old maintenance office.

Skeleton inside.

The technician died on October 23, 2077.

And they have an actual facility maintenance badge.

It's not Presidential authorization.

It's not Enclave clearance.

It's something much more mundane:

> **CONTINUITY FACILITIES ADMINISTRATION**  
> **MAINTENANCE — SITE 01**

Suddenly the players possess credentials the Enclave expedition apparently never found.

That doesn't get them into the relay chamber.

But it can get them through maintenance doors and convince some automated systems that they're supposed to be here.

Reward exploration.

# Eventually all three routes converge

Now they reach the point that stopped the Enclave.

And I would make this visually impressive.

A completely intact corridor.

No rust.

No rubble.

No bodies.

At the end stands an enormous segmented blast door.

Above it:

> **PRESIDENTIAL RELAY SITE 01**  
> **CONTINUITY NETWORK CONTROL**

The Enclave tried everything.

Cutting marks.

Explosive residue.

A bypass cable running from their equipment into the access panel.

They even removed part of the wall trying to reach the locking mechanism.

Nothing worked.

The players activate the terminal.

> **IDENTIFICATION REQUIRED**

Whatever they present produces different responses.

Enclave credentials:

> **FEDERAL AUTHORITY — CONFIRMED**

Maintenance badge:

> **FACILITY PERSONNEL — CONFIRMED**

Nothing:

> **IDENTIFICATION — UNCONFIRMED**

But all three eventually reach:

> **CONTINUITY NETWORK AUTHORITY...**

Pause.

And unlike when the Enclave stood here:

> **ONLINE**

Their radio crackles.

The same signal they've been hearing since the tower activated.

Then:

> 
> **RELAY SITE 01 AUTHENTICATED**
> 
> **EMERGENCY CONTINUITY PROTOCOL ACTIVE**

Heavy machinery starts moving inside the wall.

The Enclave cutting equipment actually **falls away from the door** as it begins retracting.

And for the first time since 2077...

**Relay Site 01 opens.**

That's where I'd stop the "dungeon" portion. From this point forward, the players are entering the part of the facility that **even the Enclave never saw**, and I'd make what they discover inside much more exploration/lore-heavy before we point them toward WMATA Dispatch.

## The only thing I would remove

I'd actually remove this from the very end:

> _"The station is waking up... A train horn... End session."_

Because you've already created a **much stronger** ending.

The Dispatch room is your "Luke sees the Death Star" moment. The players finally understand the scale of what they're dealing with. Adding another teaser after that actually dilutes the impact.

I'd end **the instant** they watch Nahk step off the train.

Don't explain anything.

Don't play a horn.

Don't cut to another location.

Just let them sit there, staring at the monitor, realizing that everything they believed about the mutant attacks was wrong.

I think that is the strongest cliffhanger in the campaign so far.
